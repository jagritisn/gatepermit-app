# Role: Approving Officer

## Who they are

Government staff responsible for reviewing entry permit requests for their office and deciding whether to approve or deny them. They work through a queue of requests as part of their regular duties, likely alongside other responsibilities — this is a tool they dip into repeatedly through the day, not a dedicated full-time task. They need to move fast without being careless: every decision they make either lets someone in or turns them away.

## What they need to see and do

- A prioritized queue of incoming permit requests, with enough at-a-glance information to triage (visitor's stated purpose, requested date/time, request age).
- Full detail on a single request when reviewing it: the visitor's simulated identity, stated purpose, and requested visit window.
- Approve, deny, or request more information from the visitor.
- See the outcome communicated to the visitor automatically — they should never need to notify the visitor by hand.
- A record of their own past decisions (what they approved/denied and, ideally, why) for accountability.

## What they must never do

- Never approve a request without the minimum required information present (identity capture, stated purpose, valid requested window).
- Never see or need real biometric or citizen-registry data — only the simulated identity captured at request time (see [`knowledge/domain/privacy-and-compliance.md`](../../knowledge/domain/privacy-and-compliance.md)).
- Never issue a pass directly without going through the request record — every pass traces back to a specific decision.
- Never lose visibility into a request once acted on; decisions should not be silently reversible without a trace.

## What success looks like

An officer clears their queue quickly and confidently, each decision is fast because the information they need is right in front of them, and if a decision is ever questioned later, they can point to exactly what they saw and what they decided.
